What is Community Detection?

Given a network (graph), community detection finds groups of densely connected nodes. The Leiden algorithm (Traag, Waltman & van Eck, 2019) is the state-of-the-art method that improves on the earlier Louvain algorithm by guaranteeing well-connected communities.

Leiden Algorithm

Three-phase iterative process:

  • Local moving — greedily move nodes to neighboring communities for maximum quality gain
  • Refinement — check each community is well-connected internally (prevents badly connected communities that plague Louvain)
  • Aggregation — collapse communities into super-nodes and repeat

CPM (Constant Potts Model)

A resolution-based quality function. Unlike modularity, CPM has a clear interpretation: communities are subgraphs whose internal edge density exceeds the resolution parameter γ.

Higher γ → smaller, denser communities.
Lower γ → larger, sparser communities.

ki→C = edges from i to nodes in C  |  ki→D\i = edges from i to rest of its community  |  nC, nD = community sizes
